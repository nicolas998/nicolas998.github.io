**Is This Rainfall Forecast Good or Bad? For Flood Forecasting, the Answer Is Scale Dependent**

Quantitative Precipitation Forecasts (QPF) from High-Resolution Rapid Refresh (HRRR) numerical weather prediction models are increasingly integrated into operational hydrological systems to extend flood warning lead times. However, the degree to which QPF spatial and temporal uncertainties propagate into streamflow predictions across varying basin scales remains poorly quantified. In this study, we evaluate HRRR-driven streamflow predictions generated by the Hillslope Link Model (HLM) framework across hundreds of USGS-gauged river basins in Iowa.

By comparing HRRR-forced streamflow forecasts against benchmark simulations driven by Multi-Radar Multi-Sensor (MRMS) Quantitative Precipitation Estimation (QPE), we systematically analyze hydrologic performance across 18 distinct forecast windows ranging from 1 to 18 hours. Our results demonstrate that HRRR QPF skill for flood forecasting is strongly scale dependent: while HRRR inputs provide valuable predictive capacity in larger drainage networks, forecast skill degrades rapidly with increasing lead times and offers marginal benefit in small-scale headwater catchments (< 1,000 km²). These findings highlight spatial and temporal thresholds where QPF errors dominate hydrological response, providing operational guidance for flash flood forecasting and ensemble streamflow modeling.
