**Identifying flash flood-prone watersheds using geomorphological and radar-derived rainfall features: a case study in Antioquia, Colombia**

Flash floods are among the most destructive hydrometeorological hazards due to their sudden occurrence, localized nature, and severe socio-economic impacts. In the mountainous region of Antioquia, Colombia (my home country), the combination of steep slopes and intense rainfall has made flash floods responsible for around 200 M USD in losses and about 1000 fatalities. Identifying which watersheds are prone to these events (which we call "flashy") is crucial for planning and implementing early warning systems, but the complexity of the processes involved makes this task highly uncertain.

In this work, we present a methodology to identify flashy watersheds using accessible geomorphological features and high-resolution radar quantitative precipitation estimates (QPEs). We analyzed 63 watersheds with documented flash flood events between 2012 and 2022, along with a validation set of about 100 randomly selected watersheds. According to our results, the slope, the basin relief, the Melton Index, the HAND index, and the precipitation intensity are key determinants of flash flood susceptibility. Our classification framework, validated through a bootstrap methodology, effectively discriminates between flashy and non-flashy watersheds, offering valuable insights for disaster risk management in flash flood-prone regions such as Antioquia.

This paper is the result of the Master thesis developed by **Laura Rodríguez Martínez**, in collaboration with Professor **Luis Fernando Carvajal-Serna** and **Manuel Mario Coy Pertuz**. You can read the paper published in *Natural Hazards* [here](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-026-08396-z).
